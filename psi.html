CANT ≠ SPREAD

Pointing the plume is not the same as widening it.

Cant rotates the plume axis. Spread opens or closes the ideal cone around that axis. A canted narrow plume can move a compact footprint far downrange; a vertical wide plume can disturb a broad region centered under the vehicle.

GEOMETRY ≠ PSI PREDICTION

The cone is a teaching surface, not a gas-dynamics solution.

Real lunar PSI depends on nozzle flow, rarefaction, shocks, surface shear, particle size and shape, packing, entrainment, granular response, vehicle motion, and validation data.

Nearby assets turn surface physics into mission design.

Landing-site spacing, sensor visibility, surface contamination, abrasion, ejecta impact, and site reuse all become operational questions once exhaust can mobilize regolith.

MODEL DEFINITION

What MissionLab actually computes

The plume is represented as an ideal right circular cone with half-angle α, canted by θ from vertical at height h. MissionLab samples rays around that cone and intersects them with a flat surface to calculate the footprint polygon, area, centroid, and downrange edge.

ray(φ) = cos(α)·axis + sin(α)·[cos(φ)·b + sin(φ)·c] surface intersection: t = h / −rayz relative loading proxy ∝ engine count × thrust setting / footprint area relative erosion tendency = loading proxy × surface-response sensitivity

EVIDENCE BOUNDARIES

NASA REPORTED

PSI can erode the surface, eject particles, obscure sensing/visibility, damage or contaminate hardware, and threaten nearby assets.

MODELED

Ideal cone geometry, footprint area, centroid offset, a normalized loading proxy, and sensitivity-based screening outputs.

INFERRED

Operational interpretation that larger disturbance and smaller hardware clearance generally increase concern.

UNKNOWN / NOT PUBLIC

Any specific future lander's private nozzle conditions, exact plume field, regolith state, flight erosion rate, ejecta distribution, or certified keep-out distance.

LIMITATIONS

What this intentionally does not do

  • No CFD, DSMC, Boltzmann, DEM, or gas-granular solver.
  • No shock structure, rarefied-flow transition, plume chemistry, or nozzle expansion solution.
  • No crater-growth, eroded-mass, particle-speed, ballistic-range, or sensor obscuration prediction.
  • No plume-plume coupling between multiple engines.
  • No claim of NASA proprietary analysis, mission telemetry, or flight qualification.
